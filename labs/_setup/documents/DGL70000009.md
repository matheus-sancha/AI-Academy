## 1. Purpose

These guidelines explain *why* Technik's corrosion-resistant overlay requirements are what they are.
They are written for design engineers specifying overlay on a new part, and for anyone who needs to
understand the intent behind an acceptance criterion before proposing a change to it.

They are guidelines, not a work instruction. Manufacturing works to `SWI70000318`.

## 2. Where overlay is specified

Overlay is specified on surfaces that are wetted by produced fluid and cannot be made from a
corrosion-resistant alloy throughout, for reasons of cost or of strength. In practice this means:

- valve and manifold bores in contact with produced fluid;
- sealing surfaces and ring grooves;
- the bore transition at welded connections.

Overlay is not specified on external surfaces, which are protected by coating.

## 3. Minimum thickness, and why it is 3.0 mm

The 3.0 mm minimum finished thickness in `SWI70000318` is not a metallurgical limit. It is the sum
of three allowances:

| Allowance | Typical | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| Dilution layer | 0.5 mm | The first deposited layer is diluted by the substrate and is not fully corrosion resistant |
| Machining allowance | 1.0 mm | Overlay is deposited oversize and machined back to the drawing |
| Service allowance | 1.5 mm | Erosion and corrosion over design life |

Specifying less than 3.0 mm therefore does not "save a little margin"; it consumes one of the three
allowances entirely. If a part cannot accept 3.0 mm, the design should change, not the thickness.

## 4. High-sand service

Where the project specification identifies a high sand content, the service allowance above is not
sufficient at the bore transition, where flow direction changes. For those parts, specify an
increased thickness at the transition rather than uniformly, and state the increased value on the
drawing.

This is the reasoning behind the header pipe bore change made under `ECN70000063`.

## 5. Why porosity matters more than it looks

A pore in the overlay is a path to the substrate. Once produced fluid reaches the low-alloy steel
beneath, corrosion proceeds under the overlay, where it cannot be inspected and where it undermines
the surrounding overlay. This is why the acceptance criteria in `SWI70000318` section 5.2 treat a
small number of small indications as acceptable and any single large one as not: the concern is
depth, not area.

## 6. Specifying inspection

When specifying overlay on a new part, state on the drawing:

- the finished thickness required for each bore zone;
- the zones, so that the work instruction's measurement points are unambiguous;
- surface roughness for each zone;
- any requirement beyond `SWI70000318`.

Where the drawing is silent, `SWI70000318` applies. Do not repeat the work instruction on the
drawing; where the two disagree, the resulting quality notification is expensive and avoidable.
