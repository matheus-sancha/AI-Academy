## 1. Purpose

This instruction defines how Technik writes controlled documents and formal write-ups, so that
anyone can read one without first learning the author's habits. It covers structure, numbering,
wording and the rules for referring to other documents.

## 2. Structure

Every controlled document has, in this order:

1. **Purpose** — what the document is for, in two or three sentences.
2. **Scope** — what it covers and, where useful, what it does not.
3. **Responsibilities** — who does what, as a table, where more than one role is involved.
4. **The body** — numbered sections, one subject each.
5. **Records** — what is recorded and where.
6. **Related documents** — by number and title.

Sections are numbered `1`, `1.1`, `1.1.1`. Do not go deeper than three levels; if you need a fourth,
the section should be split.

## 3. Wording

| Word | Means |
|---|---|
| **shall** | A requirement. Not meeting it is a non-conformance |
| **should** | A recommendation. Departing from it needs a reason, not an approval |
| **may** | A permitted option |
| **will** | A statement of fact about what happens, not a requirement |

Use them precisely and never as synonyms. Most disputes about a procedure turn out to be a
disagreement about whether a sentence was a requirement.

Write in the present tense, in the third person, and in plain language. State a measurement with its
units and its tolerance every time; "tight" and "smooth" are not acceptance criteria.

## 4. Referring to other documents

Refer to a document by **number and title** on first mention and by number thereafter. Refer to a
section by its number, not by its page.

Never reproduce the text of another document. If a criterion belongs in another document, cite it;
if it belongs in yours, state it. Duplicating a criterion in two documents is the most common cause
of two documents disagreeing.

**Industry standards are cited by title and number only.** Their text is copyrighted and is never
reproduced in a Technik document, on the intranet, or in any system that indexes Technik content.

## 5. Formal write-ups

A write-up is any formal account of a finding that will be read by someone who was not present: a
quality notification narrative, a concession request, a failure account.

A write-up shall state, in this order:

1. **What was found** — the observation, with readings and the acceptance criterion they were
   compared against.
2. **Where** — the part, serial number, operation and location on the part.
3. **How it was found** — the inspection or test, and by whom.
4. **Immediate action** — what was done at the time, including whether work stopped.
5. **What is requested** — the disposition being asked for, or that none is yet proposed.

State only what was observed. A write-up does not contain conclusions about cause unless they are
labelled as such, and it never contains instructions to its reader.
