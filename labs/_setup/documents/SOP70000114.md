## 1. Purpose

This procedure defines how a change to a released part, drawing, controlled document or CNC program
is raised, reviewed, released and implemented. Every change to a released item is made through an
Engineering Change Notification (ECN). There is no other route.

## 2. When an ECN is required

An ECN is required to change any item that has reached Released status in Teamcenter. Items that are
still In Work may be changed without one.

Typical triggers:

- a design change requested by the client or by engineering;
- a manufacturing problem that cannot be solved within the existing drawing;
- a change to acceptance criteria in a work instruction;
- a supplier change that affects fit, form or function;
- repeated quality notifications pointing at the same root cause.

## 3. States

| State | Meaning | Effect on manufacturing |
|---|---|---|
| In Work | Being drafted. Content may change | None. The existing released revision remains in force |
| In Review | Circulated for approval | None. The existing released revision remains in force |
| Released | Approved. The affected items must now be revised | The new revisions become the revisions to work to |

An ECN that is In Work or In Review changes nothing on the shop floor. This is the most common
misunderstanding of this procedure: a proposed change is not a change.

## 4. Affected items

Every ECN lists the items it changes, and for each one the revision it changes from and the revision
it creates.

| Field | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Item number | The part, drawing, document or CNC program |
| Item type | Part, Drawing, Document or CNC Program |
| From revision | The revision in force before the change |
| To revision | The revision the change creates |

Revisions run A, B, C and so on. A revision is created only by a released ECN, and an item has
exactly one Released revision at a time; earlier revisions become Superseded.

## 5. Implementation on released work orders

This section carries the most operational risk in this procedure.

**Releasing an ECN does not update work orders that have already been released.** A work order
carries the drawing and CNC program revisions that were current when its paperwork was produced, and
those values do not change afterwards.

Therefore, when an ECN is released:

1. Manufacturing engineering shall identify every released work order whose operations reference an
   affected item at a revision the ECN supersedes.
2. For each one, planning shall decide, with engineering, whether to re-issue the paperwork at the
   new revision or to complete the work order at the superseded revision under a written concession.
3. The decision shall be recorded against the work order before the affected operation starts.

Operators are not expected to detect this. An operator working to the revision printed on the
paperwork is working correctly, whatever Teamcenter says today.

> A work order whose operation references a superseded revision is not, by itself, a defect. It
> becomes one if nobody has made the decision in step 2.

## 6. Records

| Record | Where |
|---|---|
| The ECN and its affected items | Teamcenter |
| Revised items | Teamcenter, as new revisions |
| Concession or re-issue decision | Against the work order |
